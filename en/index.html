01 · Setbacks, Persuasion & Goal Pursuit

Setbacks, Persuasion & Goal Pursuit

Under Review

When an original goal can no longer be pursued, how do people recommit to an alternative goal they did not initially choose?

This work compares self-persuasion with other-persuasion to examine how people rebuild goal commitment, confidence, and sustained effort after reassignment-related setbacks.

Research Design & Current Focus

  • Three preregistered experiments
  • Student-organization role reassignment scenario
  • Hamlet theater-role reassignment scenario
  • Behavioral measures using the Charging Task and Slider Task

Research Logic

  1. Reassignment-related setback
  2. Self-persuasion
  3. Endorsement of self-generated reasons
  4. Confidence and hope
  5. Goal commitment and sustained effort

The manuscript is under review. The journal, sample sizes, and unconfirmed links are not publicly disclosed.

View the preregistration and materials on OSF

02 · Social Norms & Moral Decision-Making

Social Norms & Moral Decision-Making

In Preparation

Why does the same moral reminder affect people differently?

This research examines how moral-rule primes and achievement-motivation orientations jointly influence cheating and rule compliance.

Research Design & Current Focus

  • 2 × 2 between-subjects experimental design
  • {"Achievement-motivation orientation"=>"MAF vs. MAS"}
  • {"Priming condition"=>"moral-rule prime vs. blank-screen control"}
  • University student sample, N = 213

Research Logic

  1. Moral-rule reminder
  2. × Achievement-motivation orientation
  3. Cheating and rule compliance

The manuscript is in preparation and is not presented as a published paper; no fictional journal or preprint links are shown.

03 · AI, Organizational Change & Employee Behavior

AI, Organizational Change & Employee Behavior

Ongoing Research

Do employees interpret replacement by AI and layoffs caused by conventional organizational factors as fundamentally different events?

This project compares AI-driven and conventional layoffs, focusing on how layoff survivors reassess knowledge, job security, organizational relationships, and subsequent work behavior.

Research Design & Current Focus

  • AI-driven layoffs vs. conventional layoffs
  • Knowledge hiding and work attitudes
  • Perceived value of one's knowledge and job-security perceptions
  • The theoretical model and variable structure will be refined as the project progresses

Conceptual Pathway

  1. AI-driven vs. conventional layoffs
  2. Survivors' interpretation of the event
  3. Reappraisal of knowledge, job, and organizational relationships
  4. Knowledge hiding and work attitudes

Conceptual and ongoing. No public data, statistical results, formal hypotheses, or submission status are available yet.
